# DISCOVERY 007 — SOCIAL + ECONOMY MODEL LOCK

**Status:** LOCKED  
**Date:** 2026-09-22  
**Authority:** Creator-approved HNK-VERSE product canon  
**Depends on:** DISCOVERY 002–006 + LEGACY CONVERGENCE AUDIT V2

## 1. Design question

How do people, Agents, creatures, households, groups, institutions, settlements and Personal Verses form persistent social and economic systems inside HNK-VERSE?

The Social + Economy Model defines social identity, groups, reputation, ownership, work, services, production, trade, contracts, currency, property, institutions, governance and inter-Verse exchange.

## 2. Core principle

> **SOCIETY IS A PERSISTENT NETWORK OF RELATIONSHIPS, OWNERSHIP, OBLIGATIONS, EXCHANGE, INSTITUTIONS AND SHARED HISTORY.**

RELATIONSHIPS → TRUST / ACCESS / COOPERATION

WORK → PRODUCTION / SERVICE

OWNERSHIP → RIGHTS / RESPONSIBILITIES

EXCHANGE → VALUE / OBLIGATION

INSTITUTIONS → RULES / ROLES / SERVICES

HISTORY → REPUTATION / MEMORY / CONSEQUENCE

## 3. Social organization scale

INDIVIDUAL
→ HOUSEHOLD
→ PARTY / TEAM / CREW
→ GUILD / ORDER / INSTITUTION
→ VILLAGE
→ CITY
→ REGION
→ NATION / LARGE POLITY
→ WORLD
→ INTER-WORLD / INTER-VERSE NETWORK

Not every world requires every layer. World Creators may rename or disable local social structures.

## 4. Social actors

A social actor may be a human-controlled avatar, persistent Agent, socially relevant creature, institution acting through authorized roles or aggregate settlement/system entity where required.

Every actor requires stable identity and provenance.

Social identity remains distinct from HNK Identity and canonical authority.

## 5. Groups

Groups are first-class persistent entities, not only chat channels.

A group may have:

- stable ID;
- members;
- roles;
- permissions;
- shared history;
- shared inventory/resources;
- property;
- contracts;
- goals;
- rules;
- reputation;
- treasury;
- facilities;
- relationships with other groups.

Examples include household, expedition party, crew, Taijifu dojo, research circle, school, guild, trading company, farming cooperative, settlement council and creator collective.

## 6. Membership and roles

Membership is explicit and role-aware.

Candidate role capabilities include invite, remove, access, build, withdraw, deposit, trade, teach, govern, moderate, publish, manage property, create contracts and authorize Agents.

Membership does not imply equal authority.

## 7. Relationships

D005 already locks multidimensional relationships. D007 extends them into social systems.

RELATIONSHIP = FAMILIARITY + TRUST + RESPECT + AFFECTION/AFFINITY + FEAR + SUSPICION + OBLIGATION + ROLE + HISTORY + CURRENT_STATE

Economic/social actions may affect specific components differently.

Successful trade may increase trust without increasing affection. Unpaid debt may increase suspicion and obligation. Combat cooperation may increase respect.

## 8. Reputation

Reputation is not one global morality score.

Candidate model:

REPUTATION = SUBJECT + OBSERVER / COMMUNITY + DOMAIN + HISTORY + CONFIDENCE

Examples:

- excellent builder in Village A;
- distrusted merchant in City B;
- respected Taijifu practitioner in one dojo;
- unknown in another world.

Reputation is observer/community-relative.

## 9. Rumors and social information

A rumor may propagate with source, transmission chain, confidence, distortion, audience and timestamp.

Therefore:

POPULAR BELIEF ≠ WORLD TRUTH

REPUTATION ≠ EVIDENCE

MULTIPLE RETELLINGS ≠ MULTIPLE INDEPENDENT SOURCES

This preserves the Crônicas epistemic model and AFTERWORLD social-memory precedent.

## 10. Ownership

Ownership is a persistent domain concept.

Candidate ownership targets include items, land, buildings, rooms, machines, creatures where world rules permit, businesses, inventories, blueprints, UGC, organizations and access rights.

POSSESSION ≠ OWNERSHIP

## 11. Property permissions

Property may expose rights such as enter, view, use, take, deposit, move, modify, destroy, build, automate, invite, trade, rent, transfer and publish.

Permissions may be granted to owner, household, group, role, named identity, public or world/system authority.

This extends D004:

PERMISSION + KNOWLEDGE + SKILL + RESOURCE + TOOL + PROCESS + TIME

## 12. Commons

Worlds may support public land, shared forests, communal workshops, roads, libraries, public markets, protected zones, canonical spaces and other commons.

Not all resources should be privately owned.

## 13. Work

Work is an exchange of capability, time and responsibility.

Candidate forms:

- employment;
- contract;
- commission;
- self-employment;
- cooperative work;
- institution duty;
- household task;
- public/community service;
- autonomous Agent work.

Work output should be grounded in authoritative production/service events where possible.

## 14. Services

Economy includes services, not only items.

Examples:

- teaching;
- repair;
- transport;
- research;
- support/healing inside game fiction;
- construction;
- appraisal;
- security;
- exploration guidance;
- crafting;
- translation;
- Agent configuration;
- creature care;
- logistics.

## 15. Production

INPUT → LABOR / AUTOMATION → PROCESS → OUTPUT → STORAGE / USE / TRADE

Production may depend on skill, profession, tool, building, infrastructure, energy, material quality, time, Agent labor, creature labor and world rules.

AFTERWORLD and Nova Aurora provide strong internal precedent.

## 16. Consumption and maintenance

Economic sinks may include food/material use, maintenance, repairs, energy, transport, wages, services, construction, research, local taxes/fees, creature/Agent needs, equipment wear and institutional upkeep.

The goal is meaningful circulation, not artificial grind.

## 17. Value

VALUE = UTILITY + SCARCITY + QUALITY + LOCATION + KNOWLEDGE + DEMAND + RELATIONSHIP + WORLD RULES

Price is only one expression of value.

Objects may also have practical, social, symbolic, historical, canonical or sentimental value.

Canonical significance does not automatically imply tradability.

## 18. Exchange modes

Worlds may support barter, gift, direct sale, market sale, auction, contract, salary, rent, service fee, debt, cooperative sharing and institutional allocation.

Not every world requires currency.

## 19. Currency

Currency is a local/system instrument, not a universal HNK truth.

A world may use one currency, multiple currencies, commodity money, barter, access/reputation-based exchange or no currency.

HNK-VERSE platform identity must not depend on one in-world currency.

Any future real-money system remains architecturally separate from fictional world currencies.

## 20. Markets

Candidate market layers:

- direct player/Agent trade;
- local market stall;
- institution shop;
- village market;
- city marketplace;
- regional trade;
- inter-world exchange.

Markets may have listings, offers, stock, history, fees, logistics, reputation requirements and permissions.

NPC/Agent demand should emerge from simulated needs/goals where practical rather than infinite fake demand.

## 21. Contracts

Contracts formalize obligations.

Candidate types include delivery, building, service, escort, teaching, research, repair, transport, recurring supply, employment, rent, debt, expedition and institution/governance tasks.

Contract lifecycle:

OFFER → ACCEPT → OBLIGATION → PERFORMANCE → VERIFY → SETTLE / DEFAULT

Contract state should be event-driven and auditable.

## 22. Debt and obligation

Debt may be monetary, material or service-based.

Failure to settle may affect trust, reputation, access, future contracts, relationship history or local legal/governance systems.

World rules determine enforcement.

## 23. Businesses and organizations

A business coordinates ownership, labor, production, inventory, contracts, revenue, expenses, property and reputation.

It may be human-owned, group-owned, Agent-operated, cooperative or institution-owned according to world rules.

ZERO does not require business simulation.

## 24. Agent economy

Persistent Agents may work, consume, produce, use/own property where allowed, trade, negotiate, accept/refuse contracts, learn professions, move between jobs, form preferences and participate in institutions.

Agent actions use normal domain commands and permissions.

Agents must not receive infinite hidden resources unless explicitly defined as a world abstraction.

## 25. Creature economy

Creatures may participate through transport, gathering, farming, production, exploration, protection, companionship and ecology.

Creature labor is constrained by world rules, needs, capability and bond/relationship systems.

## 26. Settlement economy

HOUSEHOLD
→ LOCAL PRODUCTION
→ VILLAGE SPECIALIZATION
→ CITY SERVICES
→ REGIONAL LOGISTICS
→ NATIONAL POLICY
→ WORLD ECONOMY
→ INTER-WORLD TRADE

Each scale adds new simulation concerns instead of merely larger numbers.

## 27. Infrastructure

Economic activity may depend on roads, ports, storage, power/energy, water, communication, markets, workshops, farms, factories, schools, labs, transit hubs and portals.

This links economy directly to D004 world creation.

## 28. Governance and economy

Governance may define local rules for taxation, spending, zoning, property, trade, access, resource protection, infrastructure, public services and institution authorization.

Governance is a gameplay authority layer.

It never grants HNK canonical authority.

## 29. Founding and civilization

The Slime / Realist Hero / Rise of Nations design DNA becomes systemic here.

BUILD HOME
→ FORM COMMUNITY
→ CREATE SHARED FACILITIES
→ SPECIALIZE PRODUCTION
→ FORM INSTITUTIONS
→ ESTABLISH GOVERNANCE
→ EXPAND SETTLEMENT
→ CONNECT REGIONS

Founding is optional. A player may remain an artisan, scholar, explorer or martial master without governing a city.

## 30. Institutions

Institutions may include academy, Taijifu dojo, guild, library, workshop, laboratory, market, farm, practice space, government, expedition organization, creature center and creator collective.

Institutions combine membership, property, roles, knowledge, services, contracts and reputation.

## 31. Human multiplayer

Human players may visit, trade, collaborate, build together, form groups, teach, adventure, compete where world rules allow, govern organizations and share institutions.

Human interaction must respect privacy, permissions, moderation and world boundaries.

## 32. Inter-Verse interaction

Multiplayer can be modeled as controlled intersection between Personal Verses.

Candidate modes:

- visit;
- invited co-presence;
- shared institution;
- trade gateway;
- expedition instance;
- shared world;
- portal link;
- published world access.

MULTIPLAYER ≠ EVERYONE SHARES ONE GLOBAL WORLD

Personal Verse continuity remains intact.

## 33. Inter-world and inter-Verse trade

Trade between worlds may require compatible portals/access, transport/logistics, allowed item categories, exchange rules, provenance and import/export restrictions.

World Creators may forbid transfer of selected entities/resources.

This prevents one world's economy from trivially breaking another's.

## 34. UGC economy boundary

Player-created content may be shared, gifted or traded inside fictional economy if rules allow.

PLAYER-CREATED VALUE ≠ HNK CANONICAL AUTHORITY

Fictional value remains separate from any future real-money layer.

## 35. Anti-exploit principles

Economic integrity requires:

- authoritative transactions;
- idempotency;
- ownership checks;
- conservation where appropriate;
- ledger/audit trail for sensitive flows;
- abuse controls;
- no client-authoritative currency mutation;
- no Agent privileged minting;
- rollback/reconciliation tools.

Nova Aurora provides strong internal technical precedent.

## 36. Social safety and moderation

Shared spaces require block/mute/report, property access control, group moderation, content moderation, anti-griefing, rollback for protected constructions, privacy controls and explicit invitation boundaries where appropriate.

These are platform-level requirements, not optional lore systems.

## 37. ZERO Social + Economy proof

ZERO should prove only:

1. one avatar owns one HOME/land container;
2. one Agent has a persistent relationship vector with the avatar;
3. one resource can be owned;
4. one work action produces a resource/item/service result;
5. one resource can transfer between two authorized actors;
6. transfer creates an authoritative event;
7. ownership changes correctly;
8. one gift or trade affects relationship/history;
9. one simple value/price representation may exist;
10. one Agent can accept/refuse an exchange according to rules;
11. logout/login preserves ownership and relationship state;
12. no client-side action can duplicate the transferred resource.

No global market, business simulation, taxation, city economy or inter-Verse trade is required for ZERO.

## 38. Locked decisions

1. Society is modeled as persistent relationships, groups, ownership, obligations, institutions and history.
2. Groups are first-class persistent entities with roles, property, rules and shared history.
3. Reputation is domain- and observer/community-relative rather than one global morality score.
4. Rumors and reputation never become world truth automatically.
5. Ownership is separate from possession and exposes explicit permissions.
6. Worlds may support private property, commons and protected spaces simultaneously.
7. Work and services are first-class economic actions.
8. Production is grounded in inputs, processes, labor/automation and outputs.
9. Economy includes meaningful consumption/maintenance sinks without becoming grind-first.
10. Value is contextual; price is not the only form of value.
11. Currency is world-specific and optional.
12. Markets should connect simulated supply/demand where practical.
13. Contracts and obligations are event-driven and auditable.
14. Agents participate in the economy through the same permissions and domain commands as other actors.
15. Settlement/civilization economy expands by scale with new simulation concerns.
16. Governance may alter local economic rules but never HNK canonical authority.
17. Founding/governance is an optional progression path, not mandatory endgame.
18. Institutions combine social, educational, professional and economic systems.
19. Multiplayer is compatible with controlled intersections between Personal Verses rather than requiring one universal shared server-world.
20. Inter-world trade respects provenance, permissions and world import/export rules.
21. Fictional world economy remains architecturally separate from any future real-money layer.
22. Sensitive economic mutations require authoritative state, auditability and anti-duplication guarantees.
